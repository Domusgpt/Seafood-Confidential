Article brief

Seafood Confidential · Reusable commissioning record

Duplicate this file for each commissioned piece. Keep the content ID unchanged through drafts, source records, social assets, and corrections. The proposed scope below is a usable default; edit it with the contributor before work begins.

Commission

Field Entry or default
Content ID Assign SC-YYYY-NNN
Working title A concrete question or decision, not a conclusion that reporting has yet to support
Author Named person or agreed byline
Editor Named accountable human
Primary audience Seafood practitioner; explain necessary terminology for an interested general reader
Primary section On the Water / At the Farm / The Trade / Rules and Reality / At the Table / Voices
Format Reported feature / Explainer / Interview / First-person account / Opinion / Sponsored contribution
Target length Default 900–1,400 words; shorten or expand to suit the reporting
Draft due Date, time and timezone agreed with the author
Intended publication Target date only; a human schedules after approval
Compensation and expenses Exact agreed terms or explicit unpaid arrangement
Rights record Link to the accepted contributor terms and permitted media uses

The reader question

Complete this sentence: After reading, a seafood [role] should understand [decision or situation] and be able to [useful next step or better question].

Example commissioning angle: “After reading, a seafood buyer should understand the components that can sit between an ex-vessel price and a delivered price, and know which assumptions to ask a supplier to explain.” This is an article idea, not a claim that any particular price or margin has been established.

Scope and structure

Open with a specific scene, decision, or clear question. Explain the mechanism in plain language. Add evidence and the perspective of people affected. Finish with what the reader can reasonably conclude and any material question that remains open.

Proposed outline:

  1. The decision and why it matters.
  2. The relevant people, place, species, and production or trade context.
  3. The evidence and practical explanation.
  4. A competing perspective or limitation where it changes the conclusion.
  5. What a reader can use and where to read further.

Name one or two questions outside this commission so the article remains manageable. If the evidence changes the premise, revise the brief with the editor.

Reporting plan

Needed evidence or perspective Intended source Owner Due Status
Primary facts and definitions Official document, direct records, or relevant primary research Author Set at commission Needed
Working experience Named practitioner with role and interests declared Author Set at commission Needed
Material alternative view Person or evidence that can test the central account Editor and author Before review Assess relevance
Quantitative example Permissioned real figures or an explicitly illustrative calculation Author With draft Needed if used

Use SOURCE_RECORD.md for consequential claims. Record source IDs alongside the draft rather than leaving verification until the end.

Metadata and media

Record species, common/scientific name where useful, geography, jurisdiction, wild/farmed/both, and supply-chain role. For a rule, include status, relevant date, authority, and last-check time.

Choose one useful feature image. Record its owner, license or written permission, credit, caption, and alternative text. Specify any requested graphic and the data it will represent. An illustrative graphic or composite must say so.

Interests and review needs

Ask the author and guests about relevant employment, sales relationships, investments, sponsorship, and other material interests. Record proposed disclosure text. Identify any technical reviewer, right-of-response need, quote check, or contractual restriction now.

Handoff and release

The author supplies the draft, source records, unresolved questions, rights records, and suggested disclosure. An invited writer uses a native Ghost Contributor account for their own draft. The editor checks the version and records approval before a human schedules or publishes.

Agents may prepare drafts, summaries, tags, and social suggestions from approved materials. They do not contact sources or release content in the MVP. Prepare up to three social drafts only after the central article claims are stable.

Final signoff fields: approved version; editor; approval time; publication destination; outstanding issues resolved; release owner.
