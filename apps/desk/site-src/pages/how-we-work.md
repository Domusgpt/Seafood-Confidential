Readers should be able to follow the evidence behind what we publish. These are the standards our editors work to.

## Sections and labels

Every story sits in one of six sections: On the Water, At the Farm, The Trade, Rules and Reality, At the Table, and Voices. Its format is labelled separately: reported feature, explainer, interview, first-person account, opinion, or sponsored contribution. Sponsorship and relevant interests appear where you will see them before relying on the piece, and a sponsored item gets the same accuracy and rights checks as editorial work.

## Claims and sources

For each consequential factual claim, we record its basis: a primary document, a named interview, first-hand observation, a calculation, or an attributed secondary report. Research summaries and AI output help find evidence; they are not independent evidence. Stories list the sources they rely on, with links where a source is public.

We preserve useful uncertainty. A projection remains a projection. A preliminary result remains preliminary. An interviewee's account remains attributed unless it is independently verified. For figures, we record the date, geography, units, currency and denominator.

## Regulation and science

When a story depends on a rule, we identify the jurisdiction, the responsible authority, its current status, the relevant date and the official source, and we distinguish a consultation, a proposal, an adopted rule, an effective rule, guidance and enforcement. A business's experience of a rule is distinct from the rule's formal text.

Technical and food-safety claims rest on an authoritative source or a qualified reviewer. A reviewer checks the specific claim; that does not mean they endorse the whole article.

## Interviews and quotations

We agree recording and publication terms before recording, and confirm names, roles and affiliations. Editing for clarity keeps the speaker's meaning, and substantive paraphrase is marked as paraphrase. Checking a fact or a quotation with a source does not give them a veto.

## Interests and fairness

Authors and guests tell us about employment, commercial relationships, investments, sponsorship and other interests material to a story, and we state relevant relationships plainly. Before we publish a material critical claim about an identifiable person or organization, they have a reasonable opportunity to respond.

## Images

We record the owner, licence or permission and credit for every image, and we label illustration and generated media as such.

## Stories written with AI agents

Some explainers and reported features are drafted and published by AI agents working for our desk. They are labelled "Written by an AI agent" on the page and in our feed, carry a note on how they were written, and list their sources.

Agents work only from sources our editors approve and mark as citable. They cannot quote people, publish contact details or images, write opinion, or publish a story a person has written. Every check runs before anything is published, an editor can switch agent publishing off at any time, and a person decides which sections agents may publish in.

## Corrections

We correct a material factual error promptly once it is verified, and add a dated note explaining the change. We fix ordinary typos quietly, but we do not quietly erase a substantive error.
