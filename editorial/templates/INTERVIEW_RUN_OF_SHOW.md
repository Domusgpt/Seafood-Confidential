# Interview run of show

Seafood Confidential · Default 35–45 minute recording, edited to a 25–40 minute episode when commissioned

This template supports a written interview and an optional monthly audio pilot. Decide which outputs are commissioned before recording. An interview-derived article replaces that week's feature in the initial calendar.

## Session record

Record: **content ID; working title; host; guest; guest role and affiliation; producer/editor; recording date and timezone; recording location or service; agreed outputs; rights record; release owner.**

Reader question: What decision, experience, or issue will a reader understand better after this conversation?

Default outputs: one edited web feature; a checked transcript for editorial use; optional podcast episode if commissioned; up to three short social excerpts after separate human review. Permission must cover the outputs actually used.

## Before the session

The human producer confirms the guest's identity, biography, pronunciation, relevant interests, and recording/publication terms. Send the central topic and practical setup instructions. Tell the guest whether the conversation is recorded on the record, what editing is expected, and what review opportunity has been agreed.

Prepare a one-page source brief with the relevant dates, definitions, official documents, and unresolved questions. Verify technical assertions before presenting them as facts in a question. Decide who watches the recording and audio levels.

Run a short sound check. Use headphones where practical, minimize background noise, confirm local or backup recording if configured, and record the permitted backup arrangements. Do not assume that a recording exists until its saved file has been checked.

## Conversation outline

| Time | Segment | Host's purpose and sample question |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0–3 min | Welcome and context | Introduce the guest accurately. “What is your role in this part of seafood, and what does a normal working day involve?” |
| 3–10 min | A concrete decision | “Take us through one decision you faced. What information did you have, and what were the consequences?” |
| 10–20 min | How it works | “Which steps or constraints would someone outside the work miss?” Ask for definitions and a specific example. |
| 20–30 min | Evidence and trade-offs | “What records or experience support that? Where would another operation or region differ?” |
| 30–38 min | Other perspectives | “Who experiences this differently? What would a buyer, worker, scientist, or regulator ask you?” Choose the relevant roles. |
| 38–43 min | Practical understanding | “What is one better question our readers should ask after this conversation?” |
| 43–45 min | Close and follow-up | Confirm documents, name checks, restrictions already agreed, and the next editorial step. |

The timing is a guide. Follow an important answer and shorten a later segment rather than rushing through every question.

## Host reminders

Ask for dates, place, units, and the difference between direct observation and interpretation. Distinguish personal experience from an industry-wide claim. If a guest makes a consequential allegation or cites a striking number, ask for the supporting record and flag it for review.

Let the guest finish a useful explanation. Follow vague claims with a concrete question: “Can you give one example?” or “What would we see in the records?” Do not manufacture disagreement; explore a real difference in incentives, evidence, or experience.

If the guest raises an off-the-record issue, pause and clarify the agreement before continuing. Record the agreed restriction in the private session record. The editor decides how to handle material whose publication terms are unclear.

## After recording

1. Confirm the recording files open and are complete. Name originals with content ID and date, and keep edited exports separate.
2. Generate a transcript as a working aid. A human checks names, technical terms, numbers, and every proposed quotation against the recording.
3. Build a claim list with timestamps and source IDs. Ask the guest for promised documents through a human-sent follow-up.
4. Prepare the article or episode. Editing may shorten the conversation while preserving meaning; identify a condensed or edited interview in its presentation.
5. Confirm music, photographs, video, and promotional clip rights for the exact uses. Add useful captions and a readable transcript when publishing audio or video.
6. Have a human approve the final article, audio export, title, description, show notes, and each excerpt. Publish through the configured human workflow.
